# What I Brought Back in My Suitcase: A Volunteering Story from Craiova

Hello, I am **Fatma Begüm DURMAZ**.

Between **1 August and 15 September 2026**, I took part in the *Go to Romania 2* **European Solidarity Corps (ESC)** project in Craiova, Romania, through KOZ-DER and hosted by Tinerii 3D. During this experience, I had one of the most beautiful summers of my life.

## Two Different Centres, Two Different Worlds

During the project, I had the opportunity to work at two different youth centres. Our first stop, located in the city centre, mainly welcomed older age groups. There, we organised **strategy and mind games workshops** with young people. We played games that challenged us mentally while also building lasting friendships with people from many different countries around the world.

Our second centre was **Adina Home**, a youth centre located in a rural area. To get there, we travelled every day on a village bus together with local people. Every time I took that bus, I felt like a real local in Romania. I was no longer simply a tourist looking at the city from the outside; I was travelling side by side with local people to a village that perhaps even many people living in Craiova had never heard of.

## Beyond the Screens, Through the Universal Language of Play

The children came to Adina Home after school. We designed different workshops and games for them, creating a space where they could spend their free time not looking at phone or tablet screens, but **socialising, sharing and learning together**.

One of the most valuable things I gained from this experience was learning how to **overcome language barriers**. The children at the centre did not speak English, and I did not speak Romanian, but we never allowed language to come between us. Realising that we could understand each other around a game board, through a smile or a shared laugh allowed us to build a connection that went far beyond words.

## Responsibility, Adaptation and a New Version of Myself

The project contributed greatly to me not only pedagogically and socially, but also in terms of **personal independence**. Living in another country, sharing a house with volunteers from different cultures, dividing responsibilities from cleaning to cooking, and dealing with the small but memorable challenges of grocery shopping in a language I did not know made me much more mature and responsible in every part of daily life.

Looking back now, I can clearly see that **the Begüm who went to Craiova and the Begüm who returned from Craiova are definitely not the same person**.

## Thank You

I would like to sincerely thank the **KOZ-DER** family for opening this door for me and giving me the opportunity to experience such a unique journey. I am especially grateful to **Mevlide hocam**, our association president, and **Halil hocam**, whose support I always felt throughout the entire process.

I would also like to thank our host organisation, **Tinerii 3D**, for making us feel at home from the very first moment, my wonderful fellow volunteers with whom I shared my daily life, and all the children who made a place for me in their hearts.

**All the memories and experiences I collected in Craiova will continue to guide me throughout the next chapters of my life.**
