# Far from the Noise, Inside a World of Many Voices…

In the rush of daily life, we often live within invisible boundaries drawn by our surroundings and habits. Always planning the next step, we turn down our inner voice and start to believe the world is only what we see from where we stand. When I set foot in the quiet town of Puck on the Baltic coast in northern Poland, **a two-way journey** began for me: on one side, escaping the usual noise and being alone with my own mind; on the other, joining a world of many voices created by very different cultures from all over Europe.

Stepping out of my routine first made me face my own thoughts. In those first moments alone with my mind, I questioned why I do what I do and which truths really belong to me. Soon after, this inner questioning came together with the stories of people from different countries and grew into a much bigger picture.

## Cultures Meeting: Moments When the Lines on the Map Disappear

There is a huge difference between reading about or watching a country from afar and living under the same roof with people who grew up in that culture, sharing the same table and creating things together. The **intercultural evenings** held during the project turned into celebrations of traditional food, music and stories; but for me, the real cultural discovery was hidden in the natural moments outside the programme.

From conversations at breakfast to exchanges of ideas that lasted until midnight, every moment was a new window onto a different way of life. Seeing first-hand how someone from Croatia, Poland or another country approaches life, humour, problem-solving and the future broke down the patterns in my mind one by one. Realising that many habits we consider "normal" or "the only right way" in our culture mean something completely different elsewhere stretched my view of the world profoundly.

What impressed me most was the deep common ground we found within all this cultural diversity. However different our mother tongues, home towns or traditions were, seeing **how similar our worries, our excitement and our desire to achieve something are** completely removed any feeling of being strangers within a few days.

## In the Mirror of Words: Writing as an Intercultural Bridge

**(RE)WRITE YOUR TYPING**, the focus of the project, was a very special space that brought this cultural diversity together with the act of writing. Until then, I had mostly seen writing as a technical tool for conveying or putting a thought into concrete form. But while producing texts in the same workshops with young people from different cultures, I learned that **writing is actually a way to explore your own mind and to connect with others in the purest way**.

It was fascinating to see how people from different cultural backgrounds gave the same concept or question completely different souls through their words. I noticed that even the way we build a sentence carries traces of the culture we come from and our personal experiences. Sitting in front of a blank page and writing without filters first helped me put the chaos in my mind in order; then, when we shared what we had written, I witnessed first-hand that words are the strongest and most honest bridge between cultures.

## Broken Patterns and Changing Perspectives

My time in Puck taught me through experience that changing perspective is not just an abstract idea. When I began listening to people from different cultures not only through the ideas they produced but also through the historical and social background behind those ideas, I found I could look at many issues I used to judge with sharp lines in a much broader and more multidimensional way.

In quiet moments alone with my mind, I listened to my inner world; in multicultural working groups, I widened my own limits through other people's experiences. I understood that getting to know different cultures is not just about learning new traditions, but about **the ability to read the world through someone else's eyes and to re-weigh your own truths**.

## Back from the Baltic Coast: A New Compass and New Sentences

When I left the cool Baltic shore and returned to my routine, I felt that something had permanently shifted in the way my mind works. **(RE)WRITE YOUR TYPING** did not remain an event that started and ended on a calendar; instead, it gave a whole new direction to the way I think, write and connect with people. The shared creative space we built with people from all over Europe proved to me that borders exist only on maps, and that minds can always meet on common ground through the right questions and sincere communication.

Now, whether I am reading a text, weighing an idea or meeting someone new, I no longer look at things from a single centre. Being able to blend the many voices of different cultures with the courage to listen to my own inner voice is the most valuable thing this journey has left me with.

I sincerely thank **KOZ-DER** for making it possible for us to experience this deep intercultural interaction, to face our own minds and rediscover our words, and **Fundacja Autokreacja** for hosting the process so wonderfully.

**Sometimes, to truly write your own story, you first need to erase all the patterns you know, listen to the silence of your own mind and dare to watch the world through the windows of completely different cultures.**
